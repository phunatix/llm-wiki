# Leadership & Management

**Scope**: Organizational design, management practices, and leadership models — with a focus on innovation-enabling structures, handling difficult people, and facilitating productive conversations across differences.

## Core Concepts

- [[concepts/experts-leading-experts]]: Apple's model where domain experts (not general managers) lead their specialties
- [[concepts/discretionary-leadership]]: Apple's evolved model for leading at scale — own, teach, learn, delegate
- [[concepts/functional-organization]]: Single-P&L, function-based structure that aligns expertise with decision rights
- [[concepts/hard-conversations]]: Techniques for staying curious and open during difficult discussions

## Key Entities

- [[entities/Apple]]: The canonical example of a functional organizational structure at massive scale
- Steve Jobs: Architect of Apple's functional org; "experts lead experts" philosophy
- Tim Cook: Evolved Apple's leadership model for greater scale

## Key Debates

- **Functional vs. divisional**: Conventional theory says large companies need P&L-based divisions; Apple proves functional can work at $260B revenue
- **General manager vs. expert leader**: Which is more effective — training managers to lead, or enabling experts to manage?
- **Authority vs. persuasion**: "Accountability without control" — how do leaders succeed when they can't direct all the teams they depend on?

## Key Insights

- Apple's organizational secret: decision rights align with expertise, not position
- "It's easier to train an expert to manage than a manager to be an expert"
- Immersion in detail enables faster cross-functional decisions — leaders who know their domain three levels down
- Oppositional employees are often operating from fear, not bad intent — address the root cause
- "10% of platform engineering is technical, 90% is cultural change" (applies broadly to org transformation)
- Curiosity and intellectual humility are skills — they can be practiced with specific techniques
