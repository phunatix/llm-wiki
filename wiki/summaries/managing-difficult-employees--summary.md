# How to Manage a Stubborn, Defensive, or Defiant Employee — Summary

**Author**: Liz Kislik
**Source**: HBR, November 2019
**URL**: hbr.org/2019/11/how-to-manage-a-stubborn-defensive-or-defiant-employee

## One-Paragraph Summary

Oppositional employees are usually operating from fear (of change, failure, or appearing incompetent) rather than bad intent. Three tactics for getting the best from them: (1) adjust job responsibilities to leverage strengths (move a micromanaging technical expert to a purely technical role), (2) temporarily overlook style while they adjust to new circumstances, (3) consider that they may actually be right — their resistance can be alerting you to legitimate problems. Know where to draw the line when the behavior becomes damaging to the team.

## Key Claims

- Oppositional behavior is usually a mask for fear — fear of change, anticipated conflict, or appearing incompetent
- Adjusting to strengths: technical experts who struggle in management often thrive when given smaller or purely technical teams
- Temporary style tolerance: focus on quality of output, not delivery style, while person adjusts to new expectations
- Listening to the resistance: treating pushback as potentially valid information builds trust and often converts resistors into contributors
- Know the limit: when behavior becomes damaging to culture or team, the company cannot sustain it

## Key Story

At one service firm, a longtime department head who complained about every new initiative began to change when a new leader took her challenges seriously, implemented some of her "old ways," and expanded her responsibilities. She continued to challenge but became constructive rather than destructive.
