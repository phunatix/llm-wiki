# How Apple Is Organized for Innovation — Summary

**Authors**: Joel M. Podolny and Morten T. Hansen
**Source**: Harvard Business Review, November-December 2020
**URL**: hbr.org/2020/11/how-apple-is-organized-for-innovation

## One-Paragraph Summary

Apple runs on a functional organizational structure — SVPs own functions (hardware, software, design), not products — maintained intact through 40x revenue growth. The leadership model rests on three principles: deep expertise (experts lead experts), immersion in detail, and willingness to collaboratively debate. As Apple scaled, this evolved into the "discretionary leadership" model where leaders choose where to own, teach, learn, or delegate. The result: decision rights align with expertise rather than P&L accountability, enabling continuous innovation.

## Key Claims

- Apple has maintained a functional structure since 1997 — unique at its scale ($260B revenue, 137K employees)
- No general managers: only the CEO sits at the intersection of all functions for any product
- Three leadership characteristics: deep expertise, immersion in details, willingness to collaboratively debate
- "Accountability without control" — leaders are responsible for outcomes they don't directly control
- Discretionary leadership model: own / teach / learn / delegate — adapts to scale
- R&D bonuses tied to company-wide metrics, not product P&L — insulates technical decisions from short-term pressure
- VP count doubled (50→96) as company grew 8x — deliberately limited to preserve collaboration

## Key Story: Portrait Mode Camera

40+ specialist teams collaborated to build dual-lens portrait mode for iPhone 7 Plus. Involved silicon design, camera software, reliability engineering, video engineering, motion sensor hardware, and more. Decisions resolved through collaborative debate; a VP (Marineau-Mes) delayed the release a year to fix edge cases. The camera became the iPhone 7 Plus's defining feature.
