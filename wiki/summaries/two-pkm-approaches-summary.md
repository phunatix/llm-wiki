# Summary: Two Opinionated Approaches to PKM

**Source**: Crystal Lee — "Two opinionated approaches to personal knowledge management"
**URL**: https://crystaljjlee.com/blog/two-approaches-to-pkm/
**Published**: 2024-05-16 | **Clipped**: 2026-04-16

---

## What This Source Contains

A first-person account comparing two popular PKM file organization systems — **Johnny.Decimal** and Tiago Forte's **PARA method** — based on the author's own multi-year experience switching between them. Shorter and more personal than the Eric J. Ma PKM source; focuses specifically on the *tension between searchability and discoverability* in file organization.

---

## Key Takeaways

### The Central Tension: Searchability vs. Discoverability
This is the sharpest framing in the piece. The two systems optimize for fundamentally different goals:

- **Johnny.Decimal → Searchability**: You know exactly where something is because it has a permanent, memorizable address. Fast retrieval, zero ambiguity.
- **PARA → Discoverability**: You encounter things when they're relevant, because the system keeps active work front-and-center and flexible. Notes have a life beyond their folder.

Neither is universally better. The right choice depends on how your brain works and what you're organizing (files vs. notes).

### The Zettelkasten Insight
Crystal Lee briefly surfaces a key point from Zettelkasten: **linked notes are more discoverable than sequential notes**. A note filed in a hierarchical folder rarely surfaces again. A note linked to other notes resurfaces whenever you follow a related thought. This is why Zettelkasten-style PKM (and by extension, Obsidian's wikilinks) outperform flat folder hierarchies for *knowledge* even when flat hierarchies work fine for *files*.

This connects directly to why the [[concepts/llm-wiki-pattern|LLM Wiki pattern]] works: it's essentially an AI-maintained Zettelkasten — every ingested source generates linked, cross-referenced pages, not a flat archive.

### Personal Experience: Neither System is "Right"
The author's journey: JD evangelist → converted to PARA → migrated back to JD with modifications. Her conclusion: she may use **PARA for notes** (Obsidian, markdown) and **JD for files** (PDFs, downloads, static assets). A hybrid that respects the different nature of the two data types.

> "It is very much a work in progress."

This is an honest and useful corrective to PKM evangelism. No system is universally correct; personal fit matters, and most practitioners end up with hybrids.

### The JD Index as Reference Note
The author highlights JD's **Index** as a useful pattern — a reference document that maps numbers to metadata that doesn't fit in filenames. This maps onto the Zettelkasten concept of *reference notes* (distinct from fleeting and permanent notes). The Index is essentially a table-of-contents for your knowledge of a domain, not just a file locator.
