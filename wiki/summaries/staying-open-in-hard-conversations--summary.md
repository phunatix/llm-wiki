# How to Stay Open and Curious in Hard Conversations — Summary

**Author**: Mónica Guzmán
**Source**: greatergood.berkeley.edu
**Date**: April 2022

## One-Paragraph Summary

Eight practical techniques for staying curious and open in difficult conversations, grounded in the observation that intellectual humility — valuing truth over your own beliefs — is the prerequisite for productive disagreement. The techniques range from offering opinions as "snapshots" (not final positions) to listening longer, acknowledging the other side's good points, hitting reset when stuck, and saying "I don't know" when you don't. These tools help bridge divides in any high-stakes conversation.

## Key Claims

- Two foundational questions: "Are we all wrong about something?" + "Do you value truth or your own beliefs more?"
- Snapshot opinions: present views as changeable, in-progress — reduces defensiveness
- "Whose perspective wins?" → "What makes each perspective understandable?" is the key question reframe
- Listening longer: when itching to respond, ask one more question first
- Acknowledging good points on the other side models intellectual honesty and extends conversation
- "I don't know" signals non-zero-sum intent — opens space for learning
