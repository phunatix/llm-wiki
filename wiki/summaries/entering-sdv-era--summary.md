# Summary: Entering the Software-Defined Vehicle Era

**Source**: Egil Juliussen, EE Times, 2022-03-21
**URL**: https://www.eetimes.com/entering-the-software-defined-vehicle-era/

---

## One-Paragraph Summary

Juliussen provides an industry overview of the complex, expensive transition to software-defined vehicles. Modern vehicles exceed 100 million lines of code across 50+ ECUs, with product lifetimes of 10–15 years requiring continuous software maintenance across multiple model refreshes. The industry is consolidating toward domain ECUs and eventually central compute architectures, but legacy systems, real-time constraints, functional safety regulations (ISO 26262), cybersecurity legislation (UNECE WP.29), and AI certification challenges make this transition a decade-long undertaking for most OEMs.

---

## Key Insights

- **"Software-defined" means** a large majority of car functionality is implemented by software; differentiation is in the HMI, not the hardware
- **Product lifetime challenge**: Software must be maintained 10–15 years across 2–4 model updates; multiply by 10–20 models with regional variants
- **BEV as clean-sheet opportunity**: New electric vehicle platforms can start fresh instead of carrying legacy software debt
- **Domain ECU transition**: Consolidating 50+ small ECUs into powerful domain controllers; most OEMs only started this a few years ago
- **Cloud as accelerator**: AWS and Azure leading automotive cloud-based software development; SaaS functionality growing rapidly
- **Reuse is now existential**: Maximizing software platform reuse across models/generations went from low priority to required strategy
- **AI dual role**: AI is both a dependency (ADAS/AV systems need AI advances) and a solution (AI-based code generation to reduce cost/bugs)
- **Content platform war lost**: OEMs failed at in-car content platforms; Apple CarPlay and Android Auto now dominant
