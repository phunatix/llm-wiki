# Experts Leading Experts

**Domain**: Organizational Leadership / Management

**One-line definition**: Apple's foundational management principle: domain experts (not general managers) lead their respective specialties, creating organizations where the best people want to work for others they can learn from.

## Definition

At Apple, managers at every level are expected to have deep expertise in the domain they manage. A camera hardware team is led by a camera hardware expert. A software applications group is led by a software applications expert. This is not accidental — it is a deliberate organizational design principle.

The assumption: "It's easier to train an expert to manage well than to train a manager to be an expert."

## Why It Enables Innovation

1. **Expert leaders can actually evaluate work**: They know when something is wrong three levels of detail down
2. **World-class talent is attracted to expert leaders**: "If you're a great person, why do you want to work for somebody you can't learn anything from?"
3. **Decisions align with capability, not politics**: Deep knowledge leads to better bets on technology direction
4. **Insulation from short-term financial pressure**: R&D leaders at Apple are measured on company-wide performance, not product-level P&L — allowing longer-term technical bets

## Steve Jobs Quote (1984)

> "We went through that stage in Apple where we went out and thought, 'Oh, we're gonna be a big company, let's hire professional management.' We went out and hired a bunch of professional management. It didn't work at all... They knew how to manage, but they didn't know how to do anything."

## Key Implication: Decision Rights Follow Expertise

The fundamental principle of Apple's functional organization: **align expertise with decision rights** (not accountability with control as in conventional business units).

This means:
- The person making a technical decision is the person most qualified to make it
- Financial considerations are downstream, not upstream, of technical decisions
- R&D bonuses tied to company-wide performance, not individual product P&L

## Contrast with General Manager Model

| General Manager Model | Experts Leading Experts |
|---|---|
| Decision rights = seniority/position | Decision rights = expertise |
| Accountability ≈ P&L control | Accountability without full control |
| Broad, shallow knowledge of domain | Deep, narrow expertise |
| Easier to move across divisions | Harder to move — expertise is domain-specific |
