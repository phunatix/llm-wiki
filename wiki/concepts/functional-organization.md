# Functional Organization

**Domain**: Organizational Design

**One-line definition**: A company structure where senior leaders own functions (hardware, software, design, finance) rather than products or business units — with a single P&L for the entire company.

## Definition

In a functional organization, there are no general managers over products. Instead, functional experts lead their respective domains across all products. Apple's SVPs own hardware engineering, software engineering, design, operations, marketing, and retail — not iPhones, Macs, or iPads.

The only person at whose level all functions converge for any given product is the CEO.

## Contrast with Divisional/Business Unit Structure

| Functional | Divisional / Business Unit |
|---|---|
| One company P&L | Each BU has its own P&L |
| Functional SVPs (hardware, software, design) | BU GMs (iPhone Division, Mac Division) |
| Cross-functional decisions go up to CEO | BU GM makes cross-functional decisions |
| Decision rights aligned with expertise | Decision rights aligned with accountability/control |
| Unusual for large companies | Standard for large companies |

## Why Apple Defies Conventional Wisdom

Traditional management theory (Alfred Chandler, *Strategy and Structure*) holds that as firms grow, they must shift from functional to divisional structures to prevent decision congestion at the top. Apple disproves this.

The reason functional works at Apple's scale:
1. Leaders are genuine experts — they can make decisions faster because they know their domain deeply
2. Collaborative debate substitutes for GM authority — cross-functional disagreements are resolved by experts, not by one person with P&L control
3. Reputation acts as the control mechanism — teams that take big bets and succeed gain credibility for future bets

## Challenges and Mitigations

- **Congestion risk**: Solved by collaborative debate and distributed accountability without control
- **Scale risk**: Solved by the discretionary leadership model (own/teach/learn/delegate)
- **Scope expansion**: Leaders take on new areas via "learning" mode with beginner's mindset
