# Europe's AI & Cloud Dependency

**Domain**: Geopolitics / Technology Policy

**One-line definition**: Europe's critical structural dependency on US cloud infrastructure and AI models, and the vulnerability this creates if the US-EU relationship deteriorates.

## The Dependency Numbers (2025-2026)

- **70%** of cloud capacity in Europe: controlled by US providers (AWS, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud)
- **85%** of GPUs required for AI in Europe: controlled by US providers
- US hyperscalers operate more infrastructure in Germany than all European providers combined
- Electricity costs in Europe: up to **3x** US levels — structural disadvantage for AI training

## What Europe Has Built (and Hasn't)

| Category | Status |
|---|---|
| Competitive AI chips | None competitive at scale (European Processor Initiative, Axelera AI — not near NVIDIA/US-China level) |
| Competitive AI model | None at scale; Mistral is the only notable entry |
| AI regulation | Comprehensive (EU AI Act) — criticized as regulating AI that doesn't yet exist in Europe |
| AI research | Lagging behind US and China (ACL, NeurIPS dominated by US/China researchers) |

## The Nightmare Scenario

If US-EU relations escalate and the US restricts tech access (e.g., tariffs on Azure/AWS/Google Cloud):
- Losing American cloud services would "essentially paralyze the German economy and industry"
- Every major corporation depends on these services
- No European alternative exists at comparable scale

## China's Strategic Position

China has chosen **open-weight models** as a strategy (contrast with US closed models):
- DeepSeek, Kimi K2, MiniMax M2, GLM — all open-weight and competitive
- Huawei chips reducing GPU dependency on NVIDIA
- This creates a second option for Europe if US access is cut
- China's open-weight models already competitive: MiniMax M2.1 (agentic), MiniMax TTS/music

## Key Critique

> "If you can't innovate, you regulate." — Msukhareva

The EU AI Act regulates European AI that largely doesn't exist, while failing to address fundamental problems:
- Energy costs (3x US) preventing data center competitiveness
- Talent flight to US salaries
- Bureaucracy and taxes suffocating startups

## Implications for IT Professionals

German IT salary report context: AI roles not yet commanding significant premium (77K€ median for AI developer ≈ DevOps engineer level). AI is restructuring roles more than it's increasing salaries right now.
