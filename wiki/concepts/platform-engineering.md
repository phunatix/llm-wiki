# Summary

Platform engineering is the practice of designing, delivering, and evolving internal platforms that help product and application teams build and operate software more effectively. In this wiki's current sources, it is framed as a socio-technical discipline spanning capabilities, interfaces, organizational investment, operations, measurement, and increasingly the governance of AI-assisted delivery.

# Key Ideas

- Internal platforms should be evaluated by the outcomes they enable for internal users, not only by the tools they expose.
- Platform engineering includes people, process, policy, and technology, not just infrastructure automation.
- Adoption, self-service quality, platform operations, and feedback loops are central to platform effectiveness.
- Platform work evolves over time and can be assessed with maturity models, but maturity is contextual rather than an absolute ladder.
- Emerging platform responsibilities include governing AI agents, constraining AI-generated code, and embedding financial and compliance controls into delivery workflows.
- The discipline appears to be specializing into more focused roles spanning DevEx, security, observability, AI enablement, and product management.
