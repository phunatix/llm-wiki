# Summary

This source presents a CNCF platform engineering maturity model intended to help organizations assess their current state and identify adjacent improvements. It frames platform engineering as a socio-technical discipline, not just a tooling exercise, and emphasizes that maturity should be evaluated across multiple independent aspects rather than collapsed into a single overall level.

# Key Takeaways

- Platform engineering is defined as the practice of planning and providing computing platforms for internal users, spanning people, process, policy, technology, and desired business outcomes.
- The model is designed for reflection and planning, not for ranking organizations or treating the highest maturity level as inherently optimal.
- Maturity is assessed across five aspects: investment, adoption, interfaces, operations, and measurement.
- Each aspect progresses through four levels: provisional, operational, scalable, and optimizing.
- Higher maturity generally implies greater investment of funding and organizational attention, so organizations should pursue only what fits their context.
- The document explicitly positions platform work as interdependent with broader organizational transformation rather than the remit of a single team alone.

# Evidence Notes

- The introduction defines platform engineering as the practice of planning and providing computing platforms for developers and users across people, processes, policies, and technologies.
- The document states that each aspect should be evaluated independently and that organizations may exhibit characteristics from multiple levels at once.
- The model table identifies five aspects and names the four maturity levels for each.
- The text cites Martin Fowler's view that the real output of a maturity model is the list of next improvements, not the level label itself.
