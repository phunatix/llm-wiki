### Summary

In our evaluation of sovereign cloud platform vendors, we identified the most significant ones and researched, analyzed, and scored them. This report shows how each vendor measures up and helps you select the right one for your needs.

### SCPs Span Multiple Deployment Models And Build On Vendors’ Ecosystems

Sovereign cloud platforms (SCPs) aren’t plug-and-play solutions. Instead, global cloud vendors in this space offer multiple deployment models and specialized products to meet the differing sovereignty needs of their clients. Meanwhile, local cloud vendors act as sovereign-by-design providers but don’t offer the full breadth and depth of services of their global competitors. Customers don’t deliberately buy a sovereign cloud platform: They leverage a set of sovereign architectural options across different deployment models and vendors to achieve the level of cloud sovereignty they want.

Customers using this evaluation to inform a purchase decision should consider:

- **The different sovereign deployment models.** Multiple deployment models are usually available to meet customers’ sovereignty needs. Models range from public cloud with data boundaries to private cloud and air-gapped solutions. Customers should choose the deployment model that best suits their sovereignty needs, given their compliance, risk, budget, technology, and business requirements.
- **Service availability.** Not all services are available equally across all deployment models. For instance, public clouds with data boundaries offer the largest set of services and features, while sovereign private cloud and air-gapped environments offer the least. Customers will have to consider the trade-off between functionalities and their sovereignty requirements as they improve their digital sovereignty posture.
- **The technical and legal insulation of the platform.** A cloud platform isn’t sovereign simply because it has technical insulation. Self-contained services must be paired with physical, logical, and legal independence. Regional vendors usually offer sovereign-by-design services. Global vendors provide legal independence via partnerships with local vendors, joint ventures, and local entities managed by citizens or residents of the regions and countries where they want to operate as sovereign players. Customers should be aware of these different layers of insulation in order to distinguish true sovereignty from “sovereignty-washing” hype.

### Leaders

#### Google Cloud

Over the past few years, Google Cloud has secured key partnerships with local players for its sovereign cloud offerings. It supports sovereign deployment use cases in different nations.

- **Strategy.** Digital sovereignty is key to Google Cloud’s vision, which builds on three sovereign cloud platforms: public cloud with data boundaries, private cloud (Google Cloud Dedicated), and an air-gapped solution (Google Cloud Air-Gapped). The latter stands out by providing advanced services like Gemini and Vertex AI. Google Cloud’s roadmap involves delivering sovereignty as a standard feature, ensuring consistency across all platforms; this differentiates it in the sovereign cloud platforms market. However, customers have to pay for the specific sovereign controls they enable, such as premium Data Boundary packages (via a premium percentage on spend) and Cloud External Key Manager (per key/operation).
- **Capabilities.** Google Cloud excels at AI sovereign development services and applications services across all three sovereign environments. Agent Builder and DocAI integrate with Sovereign Controls in the public cloud, enabling customers to constrain data and processing to specific regions, such as the European Union (EU), while leveraging hyperscale AI capabilities. But Google Cloud’s most important feature is that it makes important cloud services consistently available across the three components of its sovereign cloud offerings.
- **Customer feedback.** Customers appreciate that they can run fully disconnected workloads; they also see Google’s AI offering as a true differentiator. However, they note that the air-gapped solution’s capabilities are limited compared with the public cloud solution.
- **Forrester’s take.** Google is an ideal choice for enterprise customers that need a full range of sovereign cloud options for their deployments.

#### Microsoft

Microsoft has recently signed partnerships with local players like Bleu and Delos Cloud to support its sovereignty strategy. It offers a broad set of sovereignty solutions across AI, security, productivity, and cloud platforms.

- **Strategy.** Microsoft’s vision is to offer sovereign controls across cloud, AI, and productivity services, including controls to meet market-specific requirements, such as the EU’s Data Boundary for data residency. It has made great progress on its cloud sovereignty roadmap. National partner clouds, such as Bleu and Delos Cloud, offer the capabilities of Microsoft 365 and Microsoft Azure in independently owned and operated infrastructure.
- **Capabilities.** Microsoft’s sovereign offering makes its capabilities available consistently for both private and public cloud. It differentiates by combining its public cloud capabilities with Data Boundary; offering Azure Arc and Azure Local with private cloud is also notable. The jewels in its crown are its sovereign container and Kubernetes services: For instance, Azure Local and Azure Arc run sovereign Kubernetes (AKS Arc or CNCF clusters) with IaC+GitOps (ARM/Bicep/Terraform, GitHub Enterprise) to provision, scale, repair, and upgrade in connected or air-gapped modes. Its sovereign database services are on par, while its connected and disconnected capabilities for Azure Local have room for improvement in terms of the number of services available.
- **Customer feedback.** Customers praise their global partnerships with Microsoft, which include productivity and collaboration tools. They express some concerns with the Key Vault solution, worrying that the presence of back doors in the code may allow the vendor to access their keys.
- **Forrester’s take.** Microsoft is a great fit for global distributed enterprises that are willing to grow their sovereign IT stack as the vendor develops its Azure Local capabilities.

#### Amazon Web Services

Amazon Web Services (AWS) is promoting a set of sovereign-by-design capabilities. It was one of the first to get started on sovereignty projects, taking a deliberate, phased approach to sovereign cloud and prioritizing depth of capability over speed to market.

- **Strategy.** AWS’ vision is “Control without Compromise”: It wants to help customers meet digital sovereignty requirements while accessing the full power of its cloud innovation. Its impressive roadmap aims to offer full-fledged capabilities in sovereign and nonsovereign environments. However, AWS’ approach to sovereignty remains fragmented, ranging from GovCloud, Top Secret, China, ESC (an independent multi-AZ, fully featured region), Dedicated Local Zones, and AI Factories. This approach reflects case-by-case, tactical moves rather than a standardized global sovereignty vision.
- **Capabilities.** AWS offers differentiated sovereign CI/CD services and DevOps support: AWS CodePipeline, CodeBuild, and CodeDeploy provide automated release orchestration with blue/green and canary deployment strategies. Full pipeline execution is available in Outposts and Dedicated Local Zones using local artifact repositories. While AWS’ application integration services are rich and complete, the sovereign nature of these services isn’t clear, resulting in an on-par score. The same is true for its app development and AI-assisted development services.
- **Customer feedback.** AWS’ customers love the availability and quality of its services. They are in the early stages of evaluating AWS’ European Sovereign Cloud, reflecting how recently it became generally available. Only one of AWS’ reference customers responded to Forrester’s outreach for this evaluation.
- **Forrester’s take.** Amazon Web Services is best suited to US organizations with strict isolation requirements as well as global organizations that need isolated regions without compromising on the services available.

#### Oracle

Oracle has one of the largest sets of sovereign cloud offerings in the market: from fully isolated regions with disconnected operations for mission-critical workloads to Exadata Cloud@Customer and Compute Cloud@Customer, which extend its commercial and sovereign cloud capabilities directly to clients’ data centers.

- **Strategy.** Oracle’s vision is to deliver a full-stack sovereign cloud with verifiable data residency, operational independence, and Zero Trust security, with consistent APIs across its public Oracle Cloud Infrastructure, EU Sovereign Cloud, Dedicated Regions, and National Security Regions. However, its sovereign cloud roadmap is evolving step by step, prioritizing the needs of customers in regulated markets; this positions the vendor in niches rather than contributing to a globally consistent portfolio of sovereign solutions for the wider market. One distinct advantage is that its sovereign options are the same price as their nonsovereign twins.
- **Capabilities.** Oracle shines with its full range of sovereign SQL and NoSQL database services, including autonomous and managed cloud databases, distributed cache, and real-time analytics; they provide flexibility for both connected and isolated environments. Fully air-gapped deployments, however, may not support some advanced cloud-managed features. Oracle’s solid sovereign AI development services span ML/DL, computer vision, NLG/NLU, speech analytics, search, and knowledge graph; all have automated lifecycle and compliance checks.
- **Customer feedback.** Customers praise Oracle for its range of sovereign options and the services available on each. However, they note large price changes from one enterprise agreement to another. Oracle did not provide reference customers for this evaluation.
- **Forrester’s take.** Oracle is a great fit for customers looking for a range of sovereign options, due to sovereignty needs in different geographies.  
	  
	View Oracle’s detailed scorecard.

#### Tencent Cloud

Tencent is the only Chinese hyperscaler that has had and still has a consistently deliberate sovereign cloud strategy. It supports this with a private cloud solution branded as Tencent Cloud Enterprise (TCE).

- **Strategy.** Tencent Cloud’s vision is to become a global leader in trusted cloud computing infrastructure with data sovereignty at its core: This transcends traditional IaaS resource building. However, it caters to meeting the data management and sovereignty needs of governments and highly regulated industries only. This leaves other organizations, perhaps with less-stringent requirements, out of scope. Its solid innovation focuses deeply on the TCE suite, but this highlights Tencent Cloud’s gap in the sovereign public cloud domain.
- **Capabilities.** Tencent Cloud offers remarkable sovereign data governance services: Its standardized governance enables full-process management and multiteam collaboration based on CI/CD standards and unified API services, while it supports both public cloud and private air-gapped environments. Its operations management services stand out compared with what other vendors in this evaluation provide. It would benefit from providing more specific support for enterprise clients.
- **Customer feedback.** Customers appreciate Tencent Cloud’s understanding of specific markets. However, they point to language barriers when it comes to the availability of English-speaking personnel. Tencent Cloud is a Customer Favorite in this evaluation.
- **Forrester’s take.** Tencent Cloud is a good option for Chinese companies in regulated industries and the public sector that are looking for a solid sovereign private cloud offering.

### Strong Performers

#### T-Systems

T-Systems was one of the first players to focus on cloud sovereignty in Europe. It bundles its services with Deutsche Telekom’s cloud services under the umbrella of T Cloud.

- **Strategy.** T-Systems’ sovereign innovations are solid and include its own sovereign public cloud, a next-generation private cloud, a hyperscaler-based sovereign cloud, and a health community cloud. Notable developments include the launch of Industrial AI Cloud, which provides resources for last-generation GPUs in Europe, and a joint go-to-market approach with AWS European Sovereign Cloud. T-Systems also aims to provide a new tech stack for its Future Cloud Infrastructure as an alternative to VMware in 2026: This will have open-source components redundant on each layer (e.g., the hypervisor) and independent of the underlying hardware. This has the potential to become an important differentiator in the market.
- **Capabilities.** T-Systems’ sovereign app development and AI-assisted development services stand out: They include integrating security standards, structured methodologies, tool support, and compliance frameworks. The T-Systems @Vantage end-to-end transformation methodology has AI tooling and offers a differentiated approach to modernization services. It also offers DevOps as a service via its STRIVE Tool Chain & AI; a sovereign AI cloud (with and without air gaps); software engineering and architecture processes and methodology; enterprise security architecture; provider identity management; digital ethics; and compliance. These and many other services are on par with other vendors in this evaluation.
- **Customer feedback.** Customers are satisfied with T-Systems’ sovereign capabilities. They highlight the complexity and lack of transparency around pricing as a drawback. T-Systems did not provide reference customers for this evaluation.
- **Forrester’s take.** T-Systems is a good choice for international organizations with core operations in Germany, Austria, and Switzerland.

#### STACKIT

STACKIT has been on the rise in the cloud and sovereignty domain since Bernd Wagner, the current CEO (and former CEO of Google Germany), took charge in November 2024.

- **Strategy.** STACKIT’s vision is to dismantle the trade-off between data protection and performance. To do this, it relies on three foundational pillars: 1) Data resides exclusively in Germany and Austria; 2) as a private company, it provides economic resilience in the face of stock market volatility; and 3) it offers technical autonomy by using open-source standards to guarantee that customers own their architecture. Its roadmap shows a strategic drive for “sovereign feature parity,” despite the limited number of products currently available. STACKIT offers a highly transparent “pay-as-you-go” model with competitive pricing.
- **Capabilities.** STACKIT delivers a comprehensive sovereign database stack hosted exclusively in EU data centers, guaranteeing GDPR, BSI C5, and ISO 27001 compliance. The vendor provides a sovereign lakehouse platform built on managed Dremio and STACKIT Object Storage. This open lakehouse architecture leverages open-source foundations like the Apache Iceberg format to ensure vendor-neutral data residency and analytical flexibility. Enterprise application integration is a core strategic focus: Specific partner solutions, such as ServiceNow, SAP Rise, and Google Workspace, can run on STACKIT, ensuring EU data residency.
- **Customer feedback.** Customers see STACKIT mainly as an infrastructure provider, noting that it doesn’t have the same number of products available as other vendors. They express concerns about unannounced maintenance downtime: For instance, virtualized GPUs can go down during the day with no prior notice, despite the SLA compliance of the vendor. Only one of STACKIT’s reference customers responded to Forrester’s outreach for this evaluation.
- **Forrester’s take.** STACKIT is a good fit for organizations in regulated sectors in Europe.

#### NxtGen Cloud Technologies

NxtGen Cloud Technologies (NxtGen) is one of the largest cloud providers in India. Its offerings in the public and private cloud space focus on the government sector as well as financial services and manufacturing companies.

- **Strategy.** NxtGen’s bold vision is to build India’s definitive sovereign cloud and AI platform for government and regulated industries. It treats sovereignty as a platform architecture principle, not a contractual overlay, and embeds data residency, operational and technological control, security, and compliance directly into infrastructure, platforms, and AI services. Rather than trying to compete with the hyperscalers’ breadth, NxtGen prioritizes depth for regulated use cases, integrating cloud, data, AI, and operations into its sovereign stack. However, the depth and breadth of its supporting services and offerings lag other vendors in this evaluation.
- **Capabilities.** NxtGen offers differentiated migration, AI applications, and AI-assisted app dev services with a curated sovereign model library, including Llama 4, DeepSeek R1, Qwen, and Kimi K2. It has industry-aligned datasets for finance, legal, and HR; prebuilt RAG/Graph RAG templates; and MLflow-registered artifacts. All artifacts are air-gapped and version-controlled in a sovereign GitLab/Harbor. Other services are on par with those of other vendors in this evaluation. Its data integration services use OpenShift with 200+ connectors and zero external dependencies and are fully air-gapped, but these are only available as sovereign services in India.
- **Customer feedback.** Customers praise NxtGen’s flexibility and responsiveness, but they express concerns that its sovereign solutions don’t scale outside India. Only one of NxtGen Cloud Technologies’ reference customers responded to Forrester’s outreach for this evaluation.
- **Forrester’s take.** NxtGen Cloud Technologies is a good fit for Indian companies in the public and financial services sectors that don’t need to scale outside the country.

#### SAP

SAP offers its sovereign cloud platform to customers that can’t leverage the common, commercially available public cloud offerings; these remain the preferred option at SAP.

- **Strategy.** SAP’s Sovereign Cloud vision revolves around offering access to SAP’s cloud innovations and AI capabilities across markets and industries; it lacks a true, forward-looking vision for its role in the broader sovereign cloud market. The SAP Sovereign Cloud benefits from SAP’s innovation and R&D investment in its broad portfolio of enterprise products: Many of these are on its sovereign cloud roadmap, such as SAP Business Data Cloud, SAP AI (enhanced through sovereign partnerships with OpenAI, Mistral, and Cohere), SAP Concur, SAP Cloud Application Lifecycle Management, SAP Customer Experience, and SAP Supply Chain Management.
- **Capabilities.** SAP’s sovereign cloud can be deployed in customer data centers, on SAP Cloud infrastructure, on hyperscaler clouds, and on its Delos Cloud subsidiary’s infrastructure. Its capabilities are solid overall. Serverless and FaaS services stand out: Serverless capabilities benefit from SAP’s enterprise security, multiruntime support, and integration with its Business Technology Platform services. For AI and RAG, serverless functions integrate with SAP AI Core to orchestrate vector stores, embeddings, retrieval pipelines, deterministic skills, and Joule-powered agents.
- **Customer feedback.** Customers appreciate being able to use the SAP Sovereign Cloud for workloads and data that they don’t want to host in the public cloud. However, they don’t see it as a general-purpose cloud yet. SAP provided only one reference customer for this evaluation.
- **Forrester’s take.** SAP’s sovereign cloud is a good fit for global SAP clients looking for a set of sovereign alternatives to public cloud deployments.

### Contenders

#### Aruba

Aruba is one of the main Italian cloud vendors: Its positioning in Europe focuses on security and sustainability.

- **Strategy.** Aruba Cloud’s vision is to be the reference cloud provider in Italy for Europewide sovereign cloud needs. It wants to enable customers to maintain maximum control over their infrastructure, data, and operations while minimizing their dependency on external providers. One key element of its value proposition is a strategic focus on using open source to drive competitiveness and interoperability for customers. Price stability is a core principle of its delivery model: Fewer billing variables and no hidden charges allow customers to manage their cloud spend without complex FinOps tooling. Its roadmap contains serverless and FaaS services, which aren’t yet available.
- **Capabilities.** Aruba offers a narrower feature set than other vendors in this evaluation. Its public cloud services balance open-source technologies and proprietary software with dedicated solutions tailored to customers’ requirements. Its migration services are on par with other players in the market: Its system migration services are based on market-standard replication software, while its professional services are tailored to migrating databases and applications, design services, transition services, and ongoing management services. Delivery of all these services is based wholly in Italy.
- **Customer feedback.** Customers consider Aruba a cost-effective and reliable infrastructure provider. However, some note areas for improvement in its UX and technical support. Aruba’s reference customers did not respond to Forrester’s outreach for this evaluation.
- **Forrester’s take.** Aruba is a solid choice for Italian and European clients that prioritize their sovereignty and infrastructure needs ahead of the range of services available.  
	  
	View Aruba’s detailed scorecard.

#### S3NS

S3NS is a joint venture between France’s Thales and Google Cloud. In December 2025, the French authorities gave it the SecNum Cloud certification — one of the most advanced digital sovereignty certifications in Europe.

- **Strategy.** S3NS aspires to deliver the best of both worlds in Europe by combining Google Cloud’s R&D with Thales’ cybersecurity heritage. It aims to provide a full hyperscale catalog in a sovereign environment; this results in a complex roadmap that might be challenging to execute in the short term. It plans to integrate its data warehouse, data lake, and lakehouse services into one service, BigLake, which it hopes will be available in 2027. A small number of alliances with European systems integrators facilitate the migration of sensitive workloads like SAP. It provides 24/7 technical assistance through French-, English-, and Spanish-speaking engineers.
- **Capabilities.** Despite the availability of Google Cloud services in the S3NS platform, S3NS makes only a limited set of capabilities generally available. Currently, customers can start building a data warehouse with BigQuery, use Cloud Storage to store data, and use Dataproc to run Spark/Hadoop clusters. Migration services lag other vendors in this evaluation: For VM migrations, S3NS works with partners like RackWare and global systems integrators to support customers when they want to migrate to PREMI3NS, S3NS’ own “trusted” cloud.
- **Customer feedback.** Customers appreciate the ease of managing compliance and security layers via the Google Cloud platform interface. They also highlight the much greater set of managed services available than from other French players. Only one of S3NS’ reference customers responded to Forrester’s outreach for this evaluation.
- **Forrester’s take.** S3NS is a good fit for international organizations in France that want a cloud vendor to enable full European sovereignty at the highest standards.  
	  
	View S3NS’ detailed scorecard.

#### IBM

IBM used to promote its sovereign cloud solution for financial services as the core of its cloud offering. It is now switching focus to its Sovereign Core based on Open Shift, a cloud-agnostic software that organizations can run in private and public clouds or in their own data centers.

- **Strategy.** Until now, IBM’s vision for its sovereign cloud offering has been unclear, resulting in below-par scores for its innovation and roadmap. However, its new focus on Sovereign Core marks a fundamental turning point. IBM promotes Sovereign Core as an AI-ready, “sovereign-by-design” software platform that helps organizations deploy and manage AI workloads while maintaining full control over their data, encryption keys, and operations within a specific jurisdiction; it can also adapt to various infrastructure configurations, from public cloud to on-premises. Sovereign Core could make a difference to customers in the near future when it is generally available.
- **Capabilities.** IBM’s service catalog lags in breadth and depth compared with other big players in the cloud space: It offers on-par capabilities in areas like compute, storage, networking, and content delivery services but relies on generic features with minimal differentiation. IBM’s database services are solid — it supports all the common data models as well as hybrid deployment approaches via SaaS or software — but it lacks the hybrid data workloads and genAI data infrastructure capabilities that would differentiate it.
- **Customer feedback.** Customers see IBM as a long-term, reliable partner and choose it for its solid delivery and Red Hat integrations. They would like to have a hyperscaler-grade service catalog. IBM did not provide reference customers for this evaluation.
- **Forrester’s take.** IBM is a good choice for enterprises operating in the financial services industry that have fewer transformational ambitions and don’t need a broad catalog of services.  
	  
	View IBM’s detailed scorecard.

#### Vultr

Vultr is a cloud computing provider that offers cloud servers and bare metal instances across more than 32 global locations.

- **Strategy.** Vultr’s vision for its sovereign cloud is to deliver better price performance for customers than the hyperscalers by offering compute and storage services at a lower cost: However, its sovereign cloud offering isn’t available on demand: It is a customized service delivered on request. Its roadmap and innovation also lag. In 2025, it raised $300 million to accelerate its growth in GPU and core cloud computing services: For CPU, it plans to target large enterprises as an extension of its multicloud and hybrid cloud strategies; for GPUs, Vultr is looking at clients that want a specialized vendor that isn’t a hyperscaler. Each of its data center regions is fully isolated, which means that everything is self-contained, and regions are connected to the global control plane.
- **Capabilities.** Vultr’s managed database services are tied to its core public cloud offering. For sovereign cloud, it supports the deployment of various SQL and NoSQL open-source databases and provides support for a unified control plane across hybrid cloud workloads through Crossplane, Headscale, and ZITADEL. Vultr’s sovereign features lack the intelligence and automation needed to streamline database management and operations. It also lags in sovereign analytics services: It doesn’t provide any native support for these within its sovereign cloud offering but enables them through Vultr Cloud Alliance partners.
- **Customer feedback.** Customers praise Vultr’s ability to offer global reach and local controls at a more affordable price point than the hyperscalers. They would appreciate a more complete range of solutions. Vultr did not provide reference customers for this evaluation.
- **Forrester’s take.** Vultr is a good fit for global organizations that need affordable compute and storage services delivered in a sovereign solution.

#### OVHcloud

OVHcloud is a European cloud vendor with global scale, which allows it to compete in the same public cloud market as the Chinese and US hyperscalers.

- **Strategy.** OVHcloud has traditionally grounded its sovereign value proposition in France, its originating country of incorporation. However, the limits of its approach have been tested recently, as Canadian authorities have issued an order to OVHcloud’s Canadian entity to access data sitting on French soil. Because this is technically possible, OVHcloud faces a legal dilemma: comply and risk violating French law or refuse and face charges of contempt in Canada. In addition, its sovereignty roadmap has been resting on its laurels: OVHcloud is struggling to provide the true differentiation and innovation that would put it on par with the sovereign offerings from its global peers.
- **Capabilities.** OVHcloud’s sovereign capabilities are limited: For instance, it doesn’t provide services in the application development space, such as architecture design, domain model design, data model design, and model/code synchronization. The same is true for AI-assisted development services, where the vendor lacks TuringBot services and capabilities to assist professional developers with business analysis and ideation, prototyping, coding, debugging, testing, deployment, and delivery.
- **Customer feedback.** Clients praise OVHcloud’s strong dedicated server performance and its highly competitive prices. They express concerns about the lack of availability of managed services. OVHcloud did not provide reference customers for this evaluation.
- **Forrester’s take.** OVHcloud is a good fit for organizations in regulated sectors that don’t need sovereignty outside Europe and want a reliable, affordable cloud infrastructure partner.  
	  
	View OVHcloud’s detailed scorecard.

#### Huawei Cloud

Huawei Cloud is a leading cloud player in China. It focuses on intelligent augmentation through innovation in broad technology areas.

- **Strategy.** Huawei Cloud’s vision for sovereignty doesn’t suggest a strong determination to play in this market at a global level, as the vendor lacks a specific strategy here. Its total public cloud revenue exceeded $55 billion in 2024 and has seen strong year-over-year revenue growth, but its business expansion in overseas markets may grind to a halt, given the emerging sovereignty concerns of organizations in the Middle East and Europe. In addition, the bans on Huawei’s AI chips in the US and the active phasing out of its network components in the EU aren’t helping Huawei Cloud’s positioning in the sovereign cloud market outside China.
- **Capabilities.** Huawei Cloud has strong capabilities in compute, networking and content delivery, and security public cloud services, but the sovereign options to leverage those services aren’t clear. The same applies to cloud development, where it provides complete product portfolios across a wide range of domains, including analytics, data integration, container and Kubernetes, application development, mobile, migration, modernization, application management, edge/hybrid management, IoT, and application integration. Overall, Huawei Cloud’s cloud capabilities are strong, but its sovereign options are its Achilles’ heel.
- **Customer feedback.** Clients choose Huawei Cloud for its public cloud services and customer support. They don’t really consider the vendor for its sovereign cloud capabilities. Huawei Cloud did not provide reference customers for this evaluation.
- **Forrester’s take.** Huawei Cloud is a good fit for organizations looking for a public cloud partner with sovereignty guardrails for the Chinese market.

### Evaluation Overview

We evaluated vendors against three categories:

- **Current offering.** Each vendor’s position on the vertical axis of the Forrester Wave graphic indicates the strength of its current offering.
- **Strategy.** Placement on the horizontal axis indicates the strength of the vendors’ strategies, including elements such as vision and innovation.
- **Customer feedback.** A halo on a vendor’s marker indicates above-average customer feedback relative to the other evaluated vendors. A double halo indicates outstanding customer feedback: We consider the vendor to be a Customer Favorite. As part of this evaluation, we speak with up to three customers of each vendor. We also consider customer input from our previous research.
  

#### Vendor Inclusion Criteria

Each of the vendors we included in this assessment has:

- **Sovereign cloud platform offerings with an international presence.** Each vendor has a standardized software foundation on which enterprises build and run complete applications that developers can freely access via a self-service interface; employs pay-per-use billing; and provides resources on demand.
- **A wide range of generally available sovereign development and infrastructure services.** Each vendor offers native development and infrastructure platform services for software development.
- **Proven enterprise adoption.** We included the vendors and products most important to global enterprise customers in at least one of the following regions: North America, EMEA, Latin America, and Asia Pacific. Client questions and primary data sources showed both usage and interest.
- **A solution available for purchase as a standalone product.** The product has its own SKU and pricing. It is not solely available as a free feature within a larger portfolio.
- **Mindshare among Forrester’s enterprise clients.** Forrester clients frequently mention the product as one they are considering prior to a purchase. We have heard about the product from our clients in the form of inquiries, advisories, consulting engagements, and other interactions over the past year. Other vendors mention this vendor as a competitor.

### Supplemental Material

#### The Forrester Wave Methodology

A Forrester Wave is a guide for buyers considering their purchasing options in a technology marketplace. To offer an equitable process for all participants, Forrester follows The Forrester Wave™ Methodology to evaluate participating vendors.

In our review, we conduct primary research to develop a list of vendors to consider for the evaluation. From that initial pool of vendors, we narrow our final list based on the inclusion criteria. We then gather details of product and strategy through a detailed questionnaire, demos and briefings, and interviews with customers (vendors may provide up to three reference customers; we also consider feedback from other customers we’ve spoken with). We use those inputs, along with the analyst’s experience and expertise in the marketplace, to score vendors, using a relative rating system that compares each vendor against the others in the evaluation.

We include the publishing date (quarter and year) clearly in the title of each Forrester Wave report. We evaluated the vendors participating in this Forrester Wave using materials they provided to us by January 27, 2026, and did not allow additional information after that point. We encourage readers to evaluate how the market and vendor offerings change over time.

In accordance with our vendor review policy, Forrester asks vendors to review our findings prior to publishing to check for accuracy. We score vendors that met our defined inclusion criteria but declined to participate in or contributed only partially to the evaluation in accordance with our vendor participation policy and publish their positioning along with those of the participating vendors.
